# employee-retention-analysis
**Questions and insights -** 

1. What is the overall employee attrition rate?
   
Answer:
The overall attrition rate was calculated as the proportion of employees who left the organization during the observed period relative to the total workforce.

Insight:
The attrition rate is materially significant, indicating that employee turnover is a non-trivial business issue with potential cost and productivity implications.

2. Which departments experience the highest attrition?

Answer:
Attrition rates were analyzed across departments to identify areas with disproportionately high employee exits.

Insight:
Certain departments exhibit consistently higher attrition than the organizational average, suggesting role-specific or management-related challenges rather than a company-wide issue.

3. How does employee tenure affect attrition?

Answer:
Employees were grouped into tenure bands to assess exit patterns over time.

Insight:
Attrition is highest among employees in their early tenure, particularly within the first few years of employment. This indicates challenges related to onboarding, role fit, or early engagement rather than long-term dissatisfaction.

4. Is compensation linked to employee attrition?

Answer:
Monthly income was segmented into bands to compare attrition behavior across compensation levels.

Insight:
Employees in lower income bands exhibit significantly higher attrition, suggesting that compensation competitiveness plays an important role in employee retention.

5. Does overtime impact employee retention?

Answer:
Attrition rates were compared between employees who regularly work overtime and those who do not.

Insight:
Employees required to work overtime show a noticeably higher attrition rate, indicating that workload and work-life balance are strong contributors to employee exits.

6. Is there a relationship between performance ratings and attrition?

Answer:
Attrition behavior was analyzed across different performance rating categories.

Insight:
Both lower-performing and high-performing employees show elevated attrition in certain cases, suggesting that attrition is influenced by multiple factors such as disengagement, burnout, or external opportunities rather than performance alone.

7. Which employee profiles are most at risk of leaving?

Answer:
Multiple variables including tenure, income, overtime status, and department were evaluated together to identify high-risk segments.

Insight:
Employees with short tenure, lower compensation, and overtime requirements represent the highest attrition risk group, highlighting the need for targeted retention strategies rather than generic HR interventions.

8. What are the key drivers of employee attrition?

Answer:
Based on combined analysis across demographic, compensation, and work-related variables, the primary drivers were identified.

Insight:
The strongest drivers of attrition include early tenure dissatisfaction, compensation constraints, workload pressure, and department-specific factors. Attrition is multi-factorial rather than driven by a single cause.

9. What business actions can be taken to reduce attrition?

Answer:
Findings from the analysis were translated into practical recommendations.

Insight:
Strengthen onboarding and engagement initiatives for early-tenure employees
Review compensation structures for high-attrition roles
Optimize workload distribution and overtime policies
Implement department-specific retention interventions
These actions are expected to reduce attrition risk and improve workforce stability.

10. How can leadership use this analysis?

Answer:
This analysis provides a structured, data-driven view of employee attrition patterns.

Insight:
Leadership can use these insights to prioritize retention investments, design targeted HR programs, and proactively manage workforce risk instead of reacting to turnover after it occurs.
